## Main Question

How can a modular 3D depth-based perception system reliably detect, track and predict the motion of dynamic obstacles in mobile robotics?

Modular in this case means the detection, tracking and prediction components can be developed, tested and replaced independently without requiring changes to the other components.
## Sub-Questions

RQ1 - What are the technical challenges of depth-based perception in dynamic robotics environments?

RQ2 - What capabilities must a dynamic obstacle perception pipeline provide for mobile robotics applications?

RQ3 - What evaluation criteria are suitable for assessing dynamic obstacle perception systems?

RQ4 - What existing methods and algorithms address dynamic obstacle detection, tracking and prediction, and how do they compare?

RQ5 - How can Kalman filters be used to develop the system?

---
## Project Goal

“To design, implement, and evaluate a modular 3D depth-based perception pipeline capable of detecting, tracking, and predicting dynamic obstacles in real time for mobile robotics applications.”

## Research Objective

To investigate whether modern world-model approaches provide practical advantages over classical perception and tracking methods for dynamic obstacle prediction in mobile robotics.

Engineering goal: build a working modular perception pipeline.

Research goal: evaluate classical vs world-model approaches.

This distinction is important academically because:
- the prototype is the engineering artifact,
- the comparison/evaluation is the research contribution.
